What do the fields mean? How does the Applicator work?
  1. Process conditions: describe what you measure and in which conditions. The Applicator ranks every technology that can do it (best match first) and shows why the others are not suitable.
  2. Sizing (optional): add flow rate, tank height or pipe size and each suitable technology is sized: pipe size, probe length, sensor range or insertion length.
  3. Inquiry: add the result to your inquiry list. Our engineers check it and confirm the exact model and order code.

1 · Process conditions

Measured variable
What you want to measure: flow (how much passes through a pipe), level (how full a tank or silo is), pressure, temperature or analysis (pH, conductivity, gas composition …).
Medium
What is in the pipe or tank: liquid (water, oil, chemicals), gas, steam, slurry (liquid with solids, e.g. mud or pulp) or bulk solid (powder, grain, sand). It rules out technologies that cannot handle it.
Temp. (°C)
The highest temperature of the medium during operation (not the ambient air). Technologies whose limit is too low are excluded.
Pressure (bar)
The highest operating pressure in the pipe or tank (gauge, i.e. above atmospheric). For pressure measurement this value is also used to choose the sensor range.
Conductivity (µS/cm)
Only for flow of liquids: how well the liquid conducts electricity. Electromagnetic flowmeters need at least 5 µS/cm. Tap water ≈ 500, demineralised water < 1, oils ≈ 0. Leave it empty if you don't know.
Hazardous area (Ex)
Tick it if the instrument is installed where explosive gas or dust can occur (refineries, gas plants, offshore). Only technologies with ATEX / IECEx versions are shown.
Hygienic design
Tick it for food, beverage or pharma processes that need cleanable, 3-A / EHEDG-certified designs.

2 · Sizing values

Flow (m³/h)
Your normal operating flow rate. Used to calculate the flow velocity and the best pipe size (DN) for each flowmeter type.
Existing pipe
The nominal size (DN) of the pipe where the meter goes. We tell you if the meter can have the same size or needs reducers.
Tank height (m)
Height of the tank or silo from the bottom to the top. Gives the measuring range, probe length or sensor range.
Nozzle (m)
Height of the mounting nozzle on top of the tank (the pipe stub the instrument is screwed or flanged onto). Typically 0.1–0.3 m.
Density (kg/m³)
Only for hydrostatic level: how heavy the liquid is. Water = 1000, diesel ≈ 840, sulphuric acid ≈ 1830.
Max. differential pressure (mbar)
Only for DP transmitters (filter monitoring, flow via orifice, level in closed tanks): the largest pressure difference between the two sides.
Pipe size (temperature / analysis)
The pipe the sensor is mounted in. Gives the insertion length of the thermometer, or the right assembly (bypass, inline, retractable) for analysis sensors.
Velocity (m/s)
Optional, for temperature: how fast the medium flows. At high velocity the thermowell needs a strength calculation.
Removable during operation
For analysis: tick it if the sensor must be taken out for cleaning or calibration without stopping the process.

Don't know a value? Leave it empty: you still get the technology ranking, and our engineers ask for the missing data.
